Delivery planning

Surrogacy hospital birth plan checklist

A hospital plan is less about scripting the birth and more about making sure the carrier, intended parents, attorneys and hospital know who to contact, what documents are expected and how newborn logistics should be handled.

Important: The gestational carrier is the patient and retains medical decision-making authority for her own care. Hospital policies and legal procedures vary, so this checklist should be adapted with the care team and attorneys.

1. Confirm the hospital and key contacts

Record the expected delivery hospital, labor-and-delivery contact, agency or case coordinator, intended-parent contacts and both attorneys. If the hospital has a social-work, patient-relations or surrogacy liaison, identify that person before delivery.

2. Confirm which legal documents the hospital needs

Ask counsel what documents should be provided before admission. Depending on the jurisdiction and facts, this may involve a parentage order, attorney letter, executed agreement or other documentation. Do not assume every hospital or state uses the same process. See the surrogacy legal process guide and legal-fee guide.

3. Discuss rooms, visitors and privacy

Hospitals differ on whether intended parents can receive a separate room, remain on the maternity floor, attend labor or delivery, or stay overnight. The carrier's preferences and hospital policy should be documented clearly, with a backup plan if space or clinical circumstances change.

4. Clarify newborn decision-making and identification

Ask the hospital how newborn bands, chart access, consent and communication are handled in a surrogacy case under the legal documents expected to be in place. The legal team should address parentage; the hospital should explain its operational process.

5. Plan feeding and newborn care conversations

Discuss feeding preferences, skin-to-skin contact, nursery use and who should receive routine newborn updates. These are preferences rather than guarantees and may change based on the carrier's wishes, newborn health or hospital policy.

6. Prepare for an unexpected delivery

Include a backup hospital contact plan, after-hours attorney numbers and instructions for what to do if delivery occurs before intended parents arrive. International intended parents should avoid travel plans that depend on one exact delivery date.

7. Plan discharge separately from birth

Confirm what the hospital expects before newborn discharge and what documents intended parents should keep with them. Medical discharge, legal parentage and issuance of a birth certificate are separate processes and may not happen on the same timeline.

Questions to ask the hospital

  • Who should receive the legal documents before admission?
  • Is there a hospital social worker or surrogacy liaison?
  • What room arrangements are possible for intended parents?
  • How are newborn bands and chart access handled?
  • What happens if the intended parents have not arrived at delivery?
  • What documents are needed for newborn discharge?
  • Who should be called if staff have questions about parentage paperwork?

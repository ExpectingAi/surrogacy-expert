What do U.S. surrogacy programs publicly charge in 2026?

There is no single national surrogacy price. Public program quotes differ because they bundle different services, risks and medical costs. The safest comparison is source-by-source and inclusion-by-inclusion.

Short answer: Public U.S. surrogacy pricing spans a wide range. Hatch Fertility currently states that surrogacy can range from about $90,000 to more than $200,000 depending on circumstances, while its own fixed-fee Peace of Mind packages are currently published at $180,000 for surrogacy only without IVF, $197,000 for one own-egg retrieval with unlimited transfers, and $239,000 for a guaranteed-birth donor-egg package. Those figures illustrate how much inclusions matter; they are not a national average.

Why these numbers cannot be turned into one “market price”

Providers define total cost differently. One quote may include IVF, legal, insurance, rematching and multiple transfers; another may exclude several of those categories. A published package also reflects that provider’s service model and risk allocation, not a transaction-weighted national average.

What to compare instead of headline totals

  • Surrogate base compensation and allowances
  • Agency or case-management fee
  • IVF, embryo creation and transfer coverage
  • Egg donor or sperm donor costs, if relevant
  • Legal fees for intended parents and surrogate
  • Insurance review, premiums and out-of-pocket exposure
  • Escrow and travel
  • Rematch policy
  • Number of embryo transfers covered
  • Lost wages, bed rest and complication-related expenses
  • Newborn medical care, which may be separate

A better budgeting method

  1. Get a written fee schedule from every provider.
  2. Mark each line item as included, excluded, estimated or contingent.
  3. Model at least one failed-transfer or rematch scenario unless the package explicitly transfers that risk to the provider.
  4. Separate surrogacy costs from donor and embryo-creation costs.
  5. Keep a contingency reserve rather than budgeting to a quoted minimum.

Why currently available surrogate profiles should not set your budget

Visible inventory can be selection-biased. Candidates with higher compensation expectations or narrower matching preferences may remain visible longer. For that reason, this site does not use the average compensation of currently available profiles as a proxy for the overall matching market.

Methodology

Sources reviewed August 7, 2026. Figures are shown as provider-published examples and attributed to the provider that published them. This page does not calculate a national average and does not infer a market-clearing price from currently available surrogate inventory.

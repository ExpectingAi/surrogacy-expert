Surrogacy for single parents: what to plan for

Single intended parents can pursue gestational surrogacy, but the medical pathway, donor needs and parentage procedure depend on the individual case and jurisdiction.

Single intended fathers

A single intended father generally needs donor eggs and a gestational carrier. Embryo creation is managed through IVF, and donor selection, genetic testing and embryo-transfer decisions should be made with the fertility clinic.

Single intended mothers

A single intended mother may use her own eggs or donor eggs and donor sperm depending on her medical circumstances. A gestational carrier may be used when carrying a pregnancy is not possible or not advisable, subject to medical evaluation and local legal requirements.

Parentage is jurisdiction-specific

Do not assume that being a single intended parent makes the legal process automatically simpler or that a pre-birth order is available everywhere. The procedure may depend on the state, court jurisdiction, genetic relationships, statutory eligibility requirements and other facts. A reproductive attorney should confirm the parentage pathway before treatment proceeds.

Matching considerations

Matching remains mutual. Some carriers may have preferences about intended-parent family structure, communication style, geography or relationship expectations. Programs should explain how those preferences affect introductions without treating any family structure as inherently easier or harder to match.

Build a support plan early

Solo parenting can make practical planning especially important. Consider who can help during travel, the hospital period, newborn care, parental leave, emergencies and the first weeks at home. This is a family-planning issue rather than a legal requirement, but it is worth addressing well before delivery.

Questions to ask your legal team

  • What parentage procedure applies to a single intended parent in this jurisdiction?
  • Does genetic relationship affect the procedure?
  • What filings are required before or after birth?
  • How will the birth certificate be handled?
  • If I live outside the delivery state or outside the U.S., are additional recognition steps advisable?

Agency Due Diligence

Questions to ask before choosing a surrogacy agency

Do not compare agencies by headline fee or advertised wait alone. Compare scope, candidate access, screening responsibility, rematch terms, financial controls and who is actually accountable at each stage.

Start with the contract and scope

Before paying a program fee, ask for the written agreement and an itemized fee schedule. Identify what is included in recruitment, screening coordination, matching, case management, insurance support, legal coordination, escrow administration and rematching. A lower headline fee is not automatically cheaper if important services are billed separately.

Candidate access

How many candidates are actually available for matching?

Ask how the program defines “available.” An applicant, a pre-screened candidate and a clinic-cleared gestational carrier are not the same thing.

How does matching work?

Some programs use a queue; others use compatibility-based matching. Ask whether intended parents can review active profiles before paying a large non-refundable fee and whether exclusivity limits outside searching.

Screening and medical clearance

What screening is completed before a profile is shown?

Clarify which records have been reviewed, whether psychosocial evaluation is complete, what remains for the fertility clinic and what happens if the clinic declines a candidate.

Rematch and withdrawal

What happens if the match does not proceed?

Ask whether rematch is included, which fees remain earned, whether new screening or legal costs apply and how long you can remain in the program.

Financial controls

Who holds and releases surrogate-related funds?

Understand whether a separate escrow provider is used, when funds must be deposited, who authorizes disbursements, what records you receive and what the agreement says about disputed payments or unused balances. Requirements can depend on state law and the structure of the program.

Legal boundaries

The agency is not a substitute for your own attorney. Confirm that you and the gestational carrier can obtain separate legal advice from attorneys licensed in the relevant jurisdiction and understand when legal clearance is required before treatment.

Case management

Who will manage your case?

Ask who your day-to-day contact is, what happens outside business hours, how provider handoffs are managed and who coordinates hospital, insurance and parentage documentation.

Do not rely on a single matching-time number

Matching time depends on current qualified candidate supply, intended-parent preferences, candidate preferences, geography, compensation expectations, legal compatibility and screening outcomes. Ask for the methodology behind any quoted timeline rather than treating it as a guarantee.

Independent surrogacy

Can you pursue surrogacy without an agency?

An independent surrogacy journey can be possible in some situations, but “without an agency” does not mean without professionals. Intended parents still need a properly screened surrogate, fertility-clinic clearance, independent legal counsel, insurance review, escrow or payment administration, and a clear coordination plan.

Short answer: Independent surrogacy can reduce reliance on a full-service agency, but it shifts more responsibility to the intended parents. The key question is not whether you use an agency; it is whether every medical, legal, financial and logistical function is still handled correctly.

What an agency normally coordinates

A full-service agency may recruit candidates, coordinate initial screening, manage matching, help assemble legal and insurance professionals, track escrow and reimbursements, coordinate with the fertility clinic, support the relationship during pregnancy and prepare for delivery. In an independent journey, those functions still exist; someone else must own them.

How intended parents may find a surrogate independently

  • A friend or family member volunteers to carry.
  • A personal or professional referral introduces a candidate.
  • Intended parents use a matching platform or dedicated search service rather than a full-service agency.
  • Parents contact multiple recruitment sources and assemble the professional team separately.

Professionals you may still need

  • Fertility clinic: final medical review, treatment protocol and embryo transfer.
  • Independent attorneys: separate representation for intended parents and the surrogate, plus parentage planning.
  • Mental-health professional: psychosocial evaluation and support as required by the clinic, law or professional standards.
  • Insurance specialist: review of pregnancy coverage, exclusions and potential additional coverage.
  • Escrow or payment administrator: structured handling of compensation and reimbursable expenses where appropriate.
  • Case-management support: optional, but valuable when intended parents do not want to coordinate every moving part themselves.

Is independent surrogacy cheaper?

It can remove or reduce some agency or case-management fees, but it does not remove the largest professional and journey expenses. Surrogate compensation, IVF, legal work, insurance, escrow, travel and medical contingencies still need to be budgeted. Compare the full journey cost rather than only the agency fee. See our surrogacy cost guide and agency-fee guide.

The biggest risks to manage

  1. Using a candidate who has not completed appropriate screening.
  2. Proceeding before independent legal review and required contract timing.
  3. Assuming existing health insurance will cover the pregnancy.
  4. Handling compensation informally without clear written terms and payment administration.
  5. Failing to define who coordinates travel, appointments, reimbursements and communication.
  6. Not planning for rematch, failed transfer or other contingencies.

Independent does not mean direct-to-transfer

Finding a willing surrogate is only the beginning. A preliminary match still needs to move through medical, psychosocial, legal and insurance steps before embryo transfer. Read our surrogate screening guide.

When independent surrogacy may fit

  • You already know the potential surrogate.
  • You are comfortable assembling and coordinating separate professionals.
  • You want more flexibility in how matching and case management are structured.
  • You understand the additional administrative work and are prepared to manage it.

When a full-service agency may be easier

  • You need active surrogate recruitment.
  • You want one team accountable for coordinating the journey.
  • You prefer less direct management of vendors, timelines and reimbursements.
  • You want established rematch and support procedures.

How long does it take to find a surrogate in 2026?

There is no fixed waiting time. Some intended parents match within weeks, while others wait many months. The biggest variables are access to active candidates, geography, budget, screening status, and how narrow the intended parents' preferences are.

Short answer

Matching time is usually driven more by the size and freshness of the available surrogate pool than by the total number of surrogates an organization claims to have recruited historically. Ask how many candidates are active now, how recently profiles were updated, and how many are already in conversation with other intended parents.

What makes matching faster?

  • Access to candidates across multiple states or providers
  • Competitive compensation expectations
  • Flexibility on geography and travel
  • Clear decisions on termination, multiples and embryo transfer
  • Embryos ready before matching
  • Fast response to profile introductions

What commonly slows matching?

  • Very narrow state requirements
  • Below-market compensation limits
  • Strict preferences on age, marital status or prior surrogate experience
  • Disagreement on termination or selective reduction
  • Insurance complications
  • Profiles that are technically listed but no longer active

Ask these five questions before paying an agency

  1. How many medically pre-screened surrogates are active today?
  2. What is the median time to a first profile presentation?
  3. What happens if I decline the first several candidates?
  4. Is the matching fee refundable if no match occurs?
  5. Can I search elsewhere at the same time?

Waiting list vs. active-profile model

A traditional waiting list places intended parents in sequence until a suitable candidate becomes available. An active-profile model lets intended parents review candidates who are already available to match. Neither model is automatically better, but they produce very different timelines and should not be compared using the same headline “average wait.”

How to find a surrogate in 2026

Intended parents may find a surrogate through a surrogacy agency, a matching platform, personal or professional referrals, or an independent journey. The practical differences are mainly candidate access, screening responsibility, coordination, fees, contracts, and who manages the case after a match.

Short answer

There is no single route that is fastest or best for every intended parent. Before treating any candidate as a real match, confirm the candidate's screening status, pregnancy history, legal eligibility, insurance review, compensation expectations, and whether both sides agree on major journey preferences.

The four main ways intended parents find a surrogate

1. Full-service surrogacy agency

An agency may recruit, pre-screen, match, and coordinate the journey. Services and screening standards vary, so confirm what the agency completes before a profile is presented and what happens after a mutual match. Before committing, compare surrogacy agency fees, candidate access, rematch terms and exclusivity.

2. Multi-provider matching platform

Some platforms display candidates connected to multiple agencies or providers. This can let intended parents compare candidates across more than one source, but profile availability does not by itself establish medical clearance, legal eligibility, or readiness to match. If current candidate access is your priority, see the guide to finding available surrogates.

3. Personal or professional referral

A friend, family member, clinic, attorney, or other professional may introduce a potential surrogate. A referral can change how a candidate is sourced, but it does not replace medical, psychosocial, legal, and insurance review.

4. Independent surrogacy

Intended parents may recruit and coordinate more of the process themselves. This changes the role and cost of an agency, but it also shifts more responsibility for assembling and coordinating the legal, medical, insurance, escrow, and support team.

How long does finding a surrogate take?

There is no reliable universal timeline. Search duration depends on candidate availability, intended-parent preferences, compensation expectations, legal compatibility, screening status, and how many candidates actually remain available and eligible through the process. For a deeper breakdown, read how long it can take to find a surrogate.

What to verify before saying yes to a match

  • Prior pregnancy and delivery history
  • Current medical and psychosocial screening status
  • State-law and clinic compatibility
  • Compensation and expense expectations
  • Insurance review
  • Views on embryo transfer, selective reduction, and termination
  • Communication expectations during pregnancy
  • Travel and delivery logistics

Should you join multiple waiting lists?

It may be possible to work with or evaluate more than one source, but contracts, exclusivity terms, deposits, duplicate profiles, and provider rules can limit that flexibility. Review those terms before paying non-refundable fees or signing agreements with multiple programs. See surrogacy agency waiting lists and whether you can work with multiple agencies.

Should you find a surrogate before choosing an agency?

Some intended parents prefer to review real candidate availability before committing to one full-service program. That model can reduce uncertainty about the search pool, but you still need to confirm who will manage screening, legal work, insurance, escrow and case coordination after a match. Compare the tradeoffs in find a surrogate first or choose an agency first.

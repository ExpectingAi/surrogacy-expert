Budget risk

What does a surrogacy rematch cost?

A rematch can create new agency, screening, legal, insurance, travel or administrative costs. The biggest variable is the provider's written rematch policy and the stage at which the original match ended.

Short answer: there is no universal rematch fee. Public fee schedules show materially different policies: some providers waive additional matching fees in specified circumstances, while others charge new fees after legal clearance, embryo transfer or other milestones.

Why rematch costs vary

A match that ends before medical screening may create a very different financial outcome from one that ends after legal contracts or embryo transfer. The provider agreement, surrogate contract, third-party invoices and escrow instructions all affect what has already been earned or spent.

Costs that may repeat

  • Agency or matching fees
  • Medical-record review and clinic screening
  • Psychological evaluation
  • Background checks
  • Independent legal counsel and contract drafting
  • Insurance review
  • Travel and appointment expenses
  • Escrow administration or account changes

For context on how provider fees are structured more broadly, see surrogacy agency fees in 2026.

When providers may waive a rematch fee

Some published programs state that no additional matching fee applies if the surrogate fails initial medical screening, while other policies depend on breach, timing, or whether embryo transfer has already occurred. This is exactly why intended parents should ask for the rematch policy in writing before paying a major non-refundable fee.

Questions to ask before signing

  1. Is a rematch included in the original agency fee?
  2. Does the answer change after medical clearance, legal clearance or embryo transfer?
  3. What if the surrogate does not pass clinic screening?
  4. What if either party ends the match for compatibility reasons?
  5. Which third-party costs must be paid again?
  6. Does unused escrow money roll forward to a new match?
  7. Are there time limits on a free or reduced-fee rematch?
  8. Does a rematch restart the waiting list or matching priority?

Rematch versus another embryo transfer

A failed embryo transfer is not automatically a rematch. The same surrogate may proceed to another transfer if medically appropriate and if both sides continue under the agreement. A rematch means the original surrogate relationship ends and a new candidate must be identified and screened.

How rematch risk affects your total budget

The safest budget is not the minimum advertised journey price. Build a contingency reserve for possible repeat professional fees, screening, legal work and travel. The 2026 U.S. surrogacy cost guide explains why contingency planning matters.

Escrow after a match ends

Unused escrow funds may remain available for future obligations or a new match, but the outcome depends on the escrow agreement and any amounts already paid. Review how surrogacy escrow works before assuming the account balance equals the refundable amount.

Surrogacy by State

Surrogacy laws in Missouri

Missouri does not have a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute. Parentage procedure, agreement enforceability, and birth-record planning should be confirmed for the specific arrangement under current Missouri law.

Framework classification: Limited / fact-dependent. The yellow classification describes the legal framework, not the quality of Missouri as a place to pursue surrogacy.

Legal framework

Missouri has general parentage law but no single comprehensive statute governing the full gestational-surrogacy process. A written agreement should not be treated as guaranteeing a particular parentage order, timing, or birth-record result.

Parentage procedure

The appropriate filing, jurisdiction, timing, and documentation depend on the facts of the case. Intended parents should confirm the expected parentage pathway with Missouri reproductive counsel before embryo transfer and again before delivery.

Agreement review

The agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical decision-making, legal costs, dispute procedures, and parentage planning. Each party should have counsel focused on that party's interests.

No county, LGBTQ+, or cross-border ranking

This guide does not rank Missouri counties, assign different legal ratings by family structure, or recommend delivery in another state. Any cross-border strategy may change jurisdiction and procedure and must be evaluated for the actual parties, treatment location, residence, and expected birth location.

Plan your next step as an intended parent

State law is only one part of a surrogacy journey. Intended parents should also compare the full budget, understand how surrogate matching works, confirm clinic readiness, and review the actual legal pathway with qualified counsel.

Surrogacy laws by state

Gestational-surrogacy law and parentage procedure can differ by state and by the facts of a particular family. Use these guides for orientation, then confirm the current law with a reproductive attorney licensed in the relevant jurisdiction.

Why there is no “best states” ranking here: legal suitability can depend on where the surrogate lives and delivers, the intended parents' residence and family structure, marital status, genetic connection, the agreement, and local court or vital-record procedures. A simple national ranking can hide those differences.

What to verify in any state

  • Whether a gestational-surrogacy agreement is permitted and what formalities apply.
  • Who qualifies as an intended parent under the relevant law.
  • Whether separate legal counsel is required or recommended.
  • How and when legal parentage can be established.
  • Which jurisdiction has authority over the parentage proceeding.
  • Birth-record and hospital documentation procedures.
  • Any rules affecting compensation, escrow, insurance, or matching programs.

Surrogacy laws by state

The colors below describe the type of legal framework in each jurisdiction. They are not a ranking of which states are “best” for surrogacy.

How to read this map: Green does not mean every arrangement automatically qualifies, and red does not mean every surrogacy-related arrangement is impossible. Parentage and enforceability can depend on the statute, agreement, family structure, marital status, genetic connection, residence, medical procedure, expected delivery location and effective dates. Reviewed August 9, 2026.
Comprehensive statutory frameworkA detailed statutory pathway governs gestational surrogacy for qualifying arrangements.
Limited / fact-dependent frameworkThe state has a narrower statute, general parentage law, case law, or significant fact-specific procedural questions.
Significant statutory restrictionsCurrent law materially restricts, voids, or prohibits relevant types of surrogacy agreements.
Recent major legal change / transitionThe state recently changed its framework or has an important effective-date transition that deserves extra attention.

What to verify in any state

  • Whether a gestational-surrogacy agreement is permitted and what formalities apply.
  • Who qualifies as an intended parent under the relevant law.
  • Whether separate legal counsel is required or recommended.
  • How and when legal parentage can be established.
  • Which jurisdiction has authority over the parentage proceeding.
  • Birth-record and hospital documentation procedures.
  • Any rules affecting compensation, escrow, insurance, or matching programs.

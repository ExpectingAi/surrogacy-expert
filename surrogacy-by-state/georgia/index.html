Surrogacy Laws in Georgia

Georgia does not currently have a comprehensive statute specifically governing gestational-surrogacy agreements. Legal planning therefore depends on existing parentage law, court procedure and the facts of the specific arrangement.

Legal note: This page is educational only. Georgia-licensed reproductive counsel should confirm the current legal pathway before an agreement is signed or medical treatment begins.

Current legal framework

Georgia's statutes do not provide a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy code comparable to jurisdictions with dedicated surrogacy acts. That means this site does not present a statewide checklist for agreement enforceability, pre-birth orders or birth-record treatment.

Parentage procedure

The appropriate parentage filing, timing and court depend on the parties' facts and current Georgia procedure. We do not label particular counties as “best,” and we do not guarantee that a pre-birth order will be available in a particular court.

Family configuration

Because Georgia lacks a dedicated surrogacy statute, this page does not claim a separate automatic procedure for married, unmarried, same-sex or single intended parents. Counsel should confirm the parentage strategy for the specific family and anticipated place of birth.

What to verify before proceeding

  • Jurisdiction and venue for any parentage proceeding.
  • How the gestational-carrier agreement should be drafted and reviewed.
  • Whether a parentage filing can occur before birth in the relevant court.
  • How the hospital and birth-record process should be coordinated.
  • Whether any new statute or appellate decision has changed Georgia law.

Georgia should be treated as a jurisdiction requiring individualized legal review, not a county-ranking shortcut.

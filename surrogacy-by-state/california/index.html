California gestational-surrogacy law

California has statutes that specifically address assisted-reproduction agreements for gestational carriers. The agreement and parentage process still need to be handled correctly for the facts of the particular case.

Legal note: This is an educational summary, not legal advice. California law, court procedure, jurisdiction, family structure, and the facts of the agreement can affect the parentage process. Confirm the current requirements with California reproductive counsel.

Statutory framework

California Family Code §§7960–7962 expressly addresses assisted-reproduction agreements involving gestational carriers. Section 7962 sets required elements for a gestational-carrier agreement and establishes procedures that are relevant to parentage proceedings.

Written agreement and independent counsel

California law requires a written gestational-carrier agreement. The statute also requires the intended parent or parents and the gestational carrier to be represented by separate, independent licensed attorneys before the agreement is executed.

Timing matters

The agreement must be executed before the gestational carrier begins medication or related medical procedures for embryo transfer, other than medical or psychological evaluation.

What the agreement addresses

The statute requires specific information in the agreement, including the identity of the intended parent or parents, information about the source of gametes subject to donor-privacy rules, and how medical expenses for the gestational carrier and newborn will be covered.

Parentage proceedings

California law provides a statutory route for intended parents to seek a judgment establishing the parent-child relationship in connection with a compliant gestational-carrier agreement. Filing procedure, timing, venue, and the exact form of the resulting order should be confirmed with counsel handling the case.

Who can be an intended parent?

California's statutory definition of an intended parent is not limited to married heterosexual couples. Family structure and genetic connection can still affect case-specific strategy and documentation, so counsel should review the exact facts rather than relying on a generic label.

What this page does not assume

  • It does not rank California as “better” than another state.
  • It does not assume every case receives the same type or timing of parentage order.
  • It does not use California surrogate compensation as a proxy for a national market rate.
  • It does not treat clinic or agency screening criteria as statutory California requirements unless the law actually says so.

Before proceeding

Have California reproductive counsel review the agreement, jurisdiction, execution timing, insurance and medical-expense provisions, and the planned parentage filing before embryo-transfer treatment begins.

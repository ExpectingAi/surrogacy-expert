Surrogacy by State

Surrogacy laws in Iowa

Iowa does not have a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute. Parentage and birth-record planning therefore require case-specific review, although Iowa administrative rules do address birth registration after a gestational-surrogate arrangement.

Framework classification: Limited / fact-dependent. This is not a ranking of Iowa as favorable or unfavorable for surrogacy.

What Iowa law addresses

Iowa law does not provide a single comprehensive statutory pathway covering the full gestational-surrogacy process. Administrative rules addressing vital records include procedures for a birth involving a gestational-surrogate arrangement, but those rules should not be treated as a guarantee of contract enforceability, a particular parentage order, or a specific pre-birth procedure.

Parentage planning

The correct parentage process depends on the facts of the arrangement and the applicable Iowa law and procedure. Intended parents should confirm jurisdiction, timing, required filings, and the expected birth-record process with Iowa reproductive counsel before embryo transfer.

Agreement review

The gestational-surrogacy agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical decision-making, legal costs, dispute procedures, and the expected parentage process. Separate legal review for the surrogate and intended parents is an important safeguard even where a specific statutory provision does not mandate the exact same process in every case.

No county or cross-border ranking

This guide does not rank Iowa counties or recommend delivery in another state. Jurisdiction and birth location can materially affect legal procedure, so any cross-border plan must be evaluated for the specific case rather than treated as a shortcut.

Surrogacy by State

Surrogacy laws in New Mexico

New Mexico has addressed gestational agreements within its parentage-law framework, while later legislation has proposed more detailed surrogacy provisions. Current applicability should be confirmed against the law actually in force for the specific case.

Framework classification: Limited / fact-dependent. This guide does not treat proposed legislation as enacted law or assume a particular parentage order is automatically available.

Current law versus proposed changes

New Mexico's parentage framework has included gestational-agreement concepts. More recent bills have proposed detailed eligibility, agreement, medical-autonomy, compensation, and parentage provisions. A bill should not be described as current law unless it has been enacted and taken effect.

Parentage procedure

The availability, timing, and form of a parentage order should be confirmed for the actual arrangement with New Mexico reproductive counsel. This guide does not assume routine pre-birth orders or a guaranteed birth-record outcome for every family structure.

Agreement review

The agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical decision-making, legal costs, dispute procedures, and parentage planning. The surrogate remains the patient and retains authority over her own medical care.

Family structure

Intended-parent eligibility and parentage analysis should be based on the current statute and the facts of the case rather than a generalized LGBTQ+, marital-status, or family-type rating.

Plan your next step as an intended parent

State law is only one part of a surrogacy journey. Intended parents should also compare the full budget, understand how surrogate matching works, confirm clinic readiness, and review the actual legal pathway with qualified counsel.

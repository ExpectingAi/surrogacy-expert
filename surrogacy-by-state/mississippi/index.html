Surrogacy laws in Mississippi

Mississippi does not provide a comprehensive modern gestational-surrogacy statute. Agreement enforceability, parentage procedure and birth-record steps should be evaluated for the specific facts and jurisdiction.

What the yellow status means: Mississippi lacks a comprehensive statutory pathway for gestational surrogacy, so this page does not rank counties, guarantee a pre-birth order, or prescribe an out-of-state delivery strategy.

Legal framework

Mississippi parentage law and relevant court authority may affect a gestational-surrogacy case, but there is no single comprehensive surrogacy act that supplies one uniform process for every arrangement.

Parentage procedure

The timing, form and jurisdiction of any parentage proceeding should be confirmed with Mississippi reproductive counsel based on the parties, genetic facts, agreement, residence and planned place of birth. A pre-birth order or particular birth-record outcome should not be assumed.

Agreement and planning

The gestational-carrier agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical autonomy, legal costs, dispute procedures and any other provisions required by applicable law or clinic practice. Separate legal advice for the parties may be important and should be confirmed for the specific arrangement.

Family structure

Do not infer a different legal result solely from whether intended parents are married, unmarried, single or LGBTQ+. The relevant parentage analysis should be based on current law and the particular facts.

Before treatment begins

Use reproductive counsel familiar with Mississippi law to confirm the agreement and expected parentage procedure before embryo transfer or other treatment steps that depend on legal clearance.

Surrogacy Laws in Nebraska

Nebraska has an express statute addressing surrogate parenthood contracts. The legal effect depends on whether an arrangement falls within that statutory definition, so Nebraska should not be described simply as a case-law state.

Educational guide only. Nebraska’s statute uses a specific definition. A Nebraska attorney should determine how it applies to the facts of a proposed arrangement.

Nebraska Revised Statute §25-21,200

Nebraska law states that a “surrogate parenthood contract” as defined by §25-21,200 is void and unenforceable. The statutory definition focuses on a contract under which a woman is compensated for bearing a child of a man who is not her husband.

Because the definition is specific, it is important not to overstate the statute as a universal rule covering every possible assisted-reproduction arrangement in exactly the same way. The facts and structure of the agreement matter.

Parentage planning

The statute also addresses rights and obligations of a biological father in a child born pursuant to a covered contract. Broader parentage, birth-record, and court procedures should be evaluated with Nebraska counsel rather than inferred from county reputation.

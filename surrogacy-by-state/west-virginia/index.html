Surrogacy by State

Surrogacy laws in West Virginia

West Virginia does not have a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy statute. Parentage, jurisdiction, agreement enforceability, and birth-record procedure should be reviewed for the specific arrangement under current law.

Framework classification: Limited / fact-dependent. The yellow classification describes the degree of statutory guidance, not whether a journey is recommended.

Legal framework

West Virginia does not provide a single comprehensive statute governing eligibility, agreement requirements, compensation, parentage, and birth records for gestational surrogacy.

Parentage procedure

The appropriate parentage filing, timing, jurisdiction, and documentation should be confirmed for the specific case with West Virginia reproductive counsel. This guide does not assume that a pre-birth order is generally available in a particular county.

Agreement review

The agreement should address compensation and expenses, insurance, medical decision-making, legal costs, dispute procedures, and parentage planning. Separate legal review for each party is an important safeguard.

No county, family-structure, or cross-border ranking

This guide does not rank West Virginia counties, assign different legal scores to LGBTQ+ families, or recommend delivery in Virginia, Pennsylvania, or another state as a default solution. Any cross-border plan can affect jurisdiction and procedure and should be evaluated for the actual parties and facts.

Plan your next step as an intended parent

State law is only one part of a surrogacy journey. Intended parents should also compare the full budget, understand how surrogate matching works, confirm clinic readiness, and review the actual legal pathway with qualified counsel.

Surrogacy Laws in North Carolina

North Carolina does not currently have a comprehensive statute specifically governing gestational-surrogacy agreements. Parentage must be planned using existing North Carolina law and current court procedure.

Legal note: This page is educational only. North Carolina-licensed reproductive counsel should review the agreement, jurisdiction and parentage strategy for the specific journey.

Current legal framework

North Carolina's General Statutes contain parentage provisions, including rules concerning children conceived through artificial insemination, but they do not provide a comprehensive gestational-surrogacy code. This site therefore does not present county-specific PBO guarantees or a statewide surrogacy checklist.

Parentage procedure

The appropriate filing, timing and court depend on the parties' facts and current local procedure. We do not label Wake, Durham, Orange, Mecklenburg or any other county as “best,” and we do not state that a pre-birth order is automatically available in those counties.

LGBTQ+ and single intended parents

The absence of a dedicated surrogacy statute means a family-specific legal analysis is important. This page does not rank regions by LGBTQ+ friendliness or promise an identical parentage procedure for every family configuration.

What to verify before proceeding

  • Jurisdiction and venue for the parentage proceeding.
  • How the gestational-carrier agreement should be structured.
  • Whether and when a parentage petition can be filed.
  • How the birth-record process should be coordinated with the hospital.
  • Whether new legislation or appellate decisions have changed the framework.

North Carolina surrogacy planning should be based on current legal advice for the specific case, not assumptions about metro areas or county rankings.

    Planning roadmap

    How to start a surrogacy journey

    A practical order of operations for intended parents — without assuming one agency, one state, one budget, or one legal pathway fits everyone.

    1

    Understand the model

    Start with the basics: gestational surrogacy, the roles of the carrier and intended parents, IVF, matching, legal agreements, insurance, and parentage. Use What Is Surrogacy? as the foundation.

    2

    Build a planning budget

    Separate agency or coordination fees, surrogate compensation and expenses, legal work, insurance, escrow or payment administration, medical costs, travel, and contingency funds. Do not treat a single headline number as a universal total. See the 2026 cost benchmark and state cost factors.

    3

    Identify the legal jurisdictions that matter

    The carrier's residence, place of treatment, place of delivery, intended parents' circumstances, and other facts can affect legal planning. Use the state-law hub for orientation, then verify the actual pathway with qualified counsel before relying on a contract or parentage strategy.

    4

    Clarify embryo and clinic readiness

    Determine whether embryos already exist, whether IVF or donor gametes are still needed, and which fertility clinic will medically evaluate and clear a gestational carrier. Medical readiness can affect matching and timing.

    5

    Compare sourcing and matching routes

    Options may include a full-service agency, a matching platform, referrals, or an independent journey. Compare what each route actually provides: recruitment, screening status, legal coordination, case management, insurance review, rematch terms, and who is responsible for each step. See How to Find a Surrogate.

    6

    Evaluate providers before signing

    Ask for written fee schedules, cancellation and rematch terms, screening procedures, conflict-of-interest disclosures, insurance-review process, and who controls funds and records. Avoid assuming that a larger provider, a higher fee, or a larger visible profile pool automatically means a better or faster outcome.

    7

    Confirm the match before treatment

    Before treatment begins, make sure medical screening, legal review, insurance planning, compensation expectations, communication preferences, travel, embryo-transfer expectations, and difficult pregnancy decisions have been addressed appropriately for the case.

    Questions to have answered before committing

    • Who is responsible for recruiting and screening the surrogate?
    • What does “screened” mean at this stage?
    • Which clinic gives final medical clearance?
    • What legal jurisdiction and parentage procedure apply?
    • How are compensation and expenses administered?
    • How is insurance reviewed and who bears uncovered costs?
    • What happens after a failed transfer, rematch, or cancelled journey?
    • Which fees are refundable, earned at milestones, or non-refundable?

    The safest starting point is not a particular provider. It is understanding which decisions belong to medical professionals, legal counsel, the parties, and the contracts involved.

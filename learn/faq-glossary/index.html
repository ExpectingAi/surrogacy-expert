    Surrogacy FAQ & glossary

    Short answers to common questions, with the important caveat that medical protocols, program rules and legal procedures are not identical across journeys.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much does surrogacy cost in the U.S.?

    There is no defensible single national price. Compare complete written budgets and separate surrogate compensation, program or matching services, IVF, legal work, insurance, travel, escrow or payment administration, and contingencies. Published quotes may bundle different services and should not be treated as equivalent without itemizing them.

    How long does a journey take?

    There is no universal total duration. Embryo readiness, carrier availability, screening, matching, legal work, insurance review, clinic scheduling, embryo-transfer outcomes, pregnancy and parentage or travel-document procedures can all affect timing.

    Does the gestational carrier have a genetic relationship to the child?

    Not through gestational surrogacy itself. The carrier does not provide the egg used to create the embryo. Traditional surrogacy is different because the person carrying the pregnancy also provides the egg.

    What is a pre-birth order?

    A pre-birth order is a parentage-related court order that may be available before birth in some jurisdictions and circumstances. It is not a universal feature of U.S. surrogacy. Availability, timing, contents and effect depend on the applicable law and facts.

    Is a gestational-carrier agreement always required?

    Written agreements are central to professionally managed gestational-surrogacy journeys and are expressly required by some state statutes. Exact legal requirements, execution timing, attorney requirements and enforceability are jurisdiction-specific. Fertility clinics also commonly require legal clearance before embryo transfer.

    Do both sides need lawyers?

    Separate legal advice is an important safeguard and is required in some statutory frameworks or programs. The exact legal requirement depends on the jurisdiction. A program or agency is not a substitute for case-specific legal advice from licensed counsel.

    Is escrow always required?

    Many journeys use an independent escrow company or another third-party payment process, and some laws or programs impose specific funding rules. The structure should be checked against the agreement and applicable law rather than assumed to be universal.

    Can same-sex couples or single intended parents pursue gestational surrogacy?

    They can pursue gestational surrogacy in many U.S. jurisdictions, but statutory eligibility and parentage procedures vary. Family structure, genetic connection and jurisdiction can affect the legal route, so current state-specific advice matters.

    Can intended parents choose embryo sex?

    PGT can reveal chromosomal sex for tested embryos, but testing, disclosure and non-medical embryo-selection policies vary by clinic and may raise ethical or legal considerations. Ask the treating fertility clinic what it offers and why.

    Who makes medical decisions during pregnancy?

    The gestational carrier remains the patient and the source of consent for her own medical care. Agreements can document expectations and decision-making discussions, but they do not transfer medical consent to intended parents.

    Glossary

    Gestational carrier / gestational surrogate

    A person who carries a pregnancy created from an embryo for intended parent or parents and does not provide the egg used to create that embryo.

    Intended parent(s)

    The person or people planning to become the child's legal parent or parents through the relevant parentage process.

    IVF

    In vitro fertilization: laboratory fertilization of an egg followed by embryo culture and, when appropriate, embryo transfer.

    Embryo transfer

    The fertility-clinic procedure in which an embryo is placed into the uterus.

    GCA / gestational-carrier agreement

    The written agreement governing the surrogacy arrangement. Required terms and formalities depend on applicable law.

    Parentage order

    A court order addressing legal parentage. Whether it is sought before or after birth, and what it accomplishes, varies by jurisdiction.

    Independent legal counsel

    An attorney advising one party's interests rather than jointly representing all participants.

    Escrow

    A third-party process for holding and disbursing funds under agreed instructions. Surrogacy payment structures vary by law, program and contract.

    Base compensation

    The core compensation amount agreed with a carrier, separate from reimbursements, allowances and conditional payments.

    Matching

    The process by which a potential carrier and intended parent or parents determine whether they want to move forward together, subject to required screening and legal or clinic clearance.

    PGT-A

    Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy. It is not automatically indicated for every IVF patient or every embryo cohort; use depends on clinical circumstances and patient preferences.

    For legal or medical decisions, use the glossary to understand the terminology, then verify the rule or recommendation with the professional responsible for your specific case.

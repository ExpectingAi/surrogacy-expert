Understanding Surrogacy

How much does surrogacy cost in 2026?

There is no single U.S. price. The total depends on what a program includes, your surrogate’s compensation and insurance, IVF needs, legal work, travel, rematching and medical contingencies.

Verified August 7, 2026. Public pricing examples below are attributed to the providers that publish them. This page does not present a national average unless a source explicitly labels its own figure that way.

What public 2026 pricing looks like

Hatch Fertility currently states in its intended-parent FAQ that surrogacy can range from about $90,000 to more than $200,000 depending on geography, surrogate experience, IVF, egg-donor needs and other variables. Its Peace of Mind page currently lists fixed-fee packages of $180,000 for surrogacy only without IVF, $197,000 for one own-egg retrieval with unlimited transfers, and $239,000 for a guaranteed-birth donor-egg package.

Do not compare headline totals without comparing inclusions. A $180,000 fixed-fee package and a lower unbundled quote can represent very different financial exposure.

Why a national average is difficult to use

Programs bundle costs differently, and each family’s medical and legal circumstances differ. A price that includes IVF, insurance, multiple transfers and rematching is not comparable with a price that covers only agency services and surrogate compensation.

For the same reason, this site does not infer a national market price from currently available surrogate profiles. Available inventory can be selection-biased because profiles with higher compensation expectations or narrower matching preferences may remain visible longer.

How to build a realistic budget

  1. Request a written fee schedule from every provider.
  2. Create an inclusion matrix: included, excluded, estimated, contingent.
  3. Separate donor/embryo-creation costs from surrogacy-only costs.
  4. Ask exactly how many embryo transfers and rematches are included.
  5. Have the surrogate’s insurance reviewed before final commitment.
  6. Model a scenario where one major contingency occurs.
  7. Keep a reserve rather than budgeting to the minimum advertised price.

What about agency fees?

Agency and case-management pricing varies substantially by provider and service model. Instead of relying on a generic “standard fee,” compare what each provider includes: recruitment, screening coordination, matching, journey management, insurance support, escrow coordination and rematching. See the agency-fee guide.

What about surrogate compensation?

Published compensation should be read as provider advertising, not a national closed-match database. For example, American Surrogacy currently advertises first-time base compensation of $55,000–$65,000+ and experienced base compensation of $70,000–$90,000+. See our verified public compensation examples.

Does state affect cost?

It can, through compensation expectations, insurance, travel, legal procedures and provider availability. But there is no defensible single price for “California surrogacy” or “Ohio surrogacy” without defining the program and inclusions. See how to compare cost by state.

    Understanding Surrogacy

    Gestational surrogacy timeline: what happens at each stage

    There is no single reliable total duration for every surrogacy journey. Matching, screening, legal work, clinic protocols, embryo availability, pregnancy and state-specific parentage procedures can each change the schedule.

    1. Preparation

    Intended parents usually begin by clarifying budget, embryo or IVF plans, preferred level of support, legal considerations and how they want to search for a surrogate. Some people choose a full-service agency, while others use referrals, multiple providers or a more independent model.

    2. Finding and evaluating a surrogate

    Matching time varies widely. Candidate availability, geography, compensation expectations, communication preferences, views on termination or multiples, insurance and legal compatibility can all affect how quickly a suitable mutual match is found.

    3. Screening and medical review

    A candidate may undergo review of prior pregnancy records, medical evaluation, psychological consultation and other program- or clinic-specific screening. The exact order and timing vary by provider and fertility clinic.

    4. Legal agreements

    Each side should have appropriate legal advice before treatment proceeds. The gestational-carrier agreement addresses issues such as compensation and expenses, medical decision-making, communication, insurance, pregnancy-related contingencies and the expected parentage process. Requirements differ by state.

    5. Clinic preparation and embryo transfer

    After the clinic's required medical and legal clearances are complete, the fertility clinic sets the medication and embryo-transfer protocol. Medication schedules, testing and pregnancy-confirmation timing are clinic-specific and should not be treated as universal.

    6. Pregnancy

    After pregnancy is established, care transitions according to the fertility clinic and obstetric team's plan. Intended-parent participation, travel, appointment attendance and communication should follow the expectations agreed with the surrogate.

    7. Parentage planning

    The legal parentage process depends on the governing state law and the facts of the case. Some jurisdictions provide a pre-birth route, others use post-birth procedures, and filing requirements can differ by court and family configuration. Parentage should be planned with qualified counsel rather than assumed from a generic timeline.

    8. Birth and post-birth steps

    Hospital coordination, birth-record documentation and any remaining court or administrative steps depend on the legal plan, hospital procedures and jurisdiction. International intended parents may also need separate immigration or citizenship planning.

    What can extend the journey?

    Common sources of delay include a longer search for a mutually suitable surrogate, additional medical review, rematching, embryo availability, unsuccessful transfers, insurance issues, legal complexity and pregnancy-related events. None of these has a reliable universal duration.

    A useful timeline is a sequence of dependencies, not a promise of a fixed number of months.

How to find available surrogates in 2026

Intended parents can search through a full-service agency, several providers, dedicated search services, or multi-provider profile platforms. The important question is what candidate pool is actually available and how thoroughly each candidate has been screened.

Updated August 2026. Candidate availability changes continuously.

Quick answer

There is no single definition of an “available surrogate.” A profile may be newly recruited, initially screened, psychologically screened, medically reviewed, or fully cleared. Before treating any profile as a real match option, intended parents should verify the candidate's current status and screening stage.

Four common ways to search

1. One full-service agency

You sign with an agency and consider candidates from that agency's recruitment pipeline. This can simplify coordination but limits the initial search to that provider's pool. Compare agency fees and inclusions before committing.

2. Contact several agencies

Parents can compare availability across providers, but should review exclusivity terms, application fees, duplicate screening, and differences in candidate standards. Read whether you can work with multiple surrogacy agencies.

3. Dedicated surrogate-search services

Some services focus primarily on locating a potential candidate before the full journey-management structure is finalized.

4. Multi-provider profile platforms

Some platforms display profiles or availability from multiple providers, allowing parents to compare a broader set of candidates before deciding how the journey will be managed.

Why an availability list can mislead

A list of profiles that are still available is not automatically a representative sample of the overall matching market. Candidates who match quickly disappear from inventory sooner, while candidates with narrower preferences, higher compensation expectations, or other limiting factors may remain visible longer. That creates a form of survivorship bias.

Before pursuing a profile

  • Confirm that the profile is current and the candidate is still accepting matches.
  • Confirm location and whether the delivery state is workable for your legal circumstances.
  • Confirm compensation expectations and major matching preferences.
  • Ask what screening has already been completed and what remains.
  • Ask whether the candidate is speaking with other intended parents.
  • Confirm who will coordinate legal, insurance, escrow, clinic clearance, and case management if you match.

Can you find the surrogate first and choose an agency later?

Some matching models are structured this way. It can allow parents to evaluate candidate availability before committing to a full-service provider. Regardless of the model, qualified legal, medical, insurance, escrow, and case-management support is still needed before proceeding. See find a surrogate first or choose an agency first.

What if an agency has no candidates for you?

Ask about recruitment volume, how many families are ahead of you, whether you can search elsewhere, and what happens to fees if you match outside the program. The guide what if your agency has no available surrogates? covers the decision points in more detail.

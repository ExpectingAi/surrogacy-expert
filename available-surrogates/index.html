How to find available surrogates in 2026

Intended parents can search through a traditional agency, multiple agencies, referral networks, or broader matching platforms. The key difference is how large a candidate pool you can access before committing.

Updated August 2026. Candidate availability changes continuously.

Quick answer

Most intended parents find a surrogate through a specialized agency or matching service. Traditional agencies generally match from their own candidate pool. Broader search models can expose intended parents to candidates sourced from multiple agencies or programs, which may reduce dependence on a single waiting list.

Four common ways to search

1. One full-service agency

You sign with an agency and wait for candidates that fit your criteria. This offers a simple, managed process but limits your search to that agency's recruitment pipeline.

2. Contact several agencies

Parents can compare availability across agencies, although application fees, duplicate work, contractual restrictions, and different screening standards can make this cumbersome.

3. Dedicated surrogate-search services

Some services focus primarily on sourcing a candidate and allow parents to decide on broader case-management arrangements after a potential match is identified.

4. Multi-provider profile platforms

Some online platforms aggregate surrogate profiles or availability from multiple providers. This can make it easier to compare options before choosing who will manage the full journey.

What “available” should mean

Always ask exactly what stage the candidate has completed. A listed profile may be newly recruited, prescreened, psychologically screened, medically reviewed, or fully cleared. Those stages are not equivalent.

Before pursuing a profile

  • Confirm location and whether the state is appropriate for your legal situation.
  • Confirm compensation expectations and major preferences.
  • Ask what screening has already been completed.
  • Ask whether the candidate is actively speaking with other intended parents.
  • Confirm who will coordinate legal, insurance, escrow, clinic clearance, and case management if you match.

Can you find the surrogate first and choose an agency later?

Yes, some matching models are structured this way. It can be useful for parents who do not want to pay a large agency fee before understanding candidate availability. However, parents still need qualified legal, medical, insurance, escrow, and journey-management support before proceeding.

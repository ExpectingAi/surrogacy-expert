Surrogacy for international intended parents

International journeys require coordinated planning across U.S. parentage law and the intended parents' home-country rules on recognition, citizenship, nationality and travel documents.

Plan both legal systems from the beginning

U.S. counsel should address the gestational-carrier agreement and the parentage procedure in the relevant state or jurisdiction. Home-country counsel should separately confirm how that parentage will be recognized and what documentation will be needed for citizenship, nationality, residency or entry.

State selection is case-specific

There is no single “best” U.S. state for every international family. Relevant factors include statutory eligibility, jurisdictional connections, the parentage procedure, delivery planning, vital-records rules and how the resulting documents will be treated abroad.

Citizenship and travel documents require current advice

Do not assume that birthplace alone resolves every citizenship, parentage, passport or home-country recognition question in a surrogacy case. Requirements are fact-specific and may involve different U.S. and foreign authorities. Confirm the current route with qualified immigration or nationality counsel, reproductive counsel and the relevant government authorities before making fixed travel plans.

Practical issues to resolve before delivery

  • Which court or state will handle parentage?
  • What will the home country require to recognize the U.S. documents?
  • Which authority will issue the child's passport or other travel document?
  • Will translations, apostilles or authentications be needed?
  • What hospital documents should be prepared before delivery?
  • How flexible should return travel remain while documents are pending?

Plan the post-birth document sequence

Hospital discharge, parentage documentation, birth-record issuance and passport or travel-document processing can follow different timelines. Review the traveling home after a U.S. surrogacy birth guide for a practical document checklist and current U.S. government sources to verify before travel.

Build a team that understands cross-border cases

Ask prospective attorneys, agencies and coordinators about actual experience with international families and with your specific home country. The important question is not the size of a provider's international marketing program, but whether the team understands the legal and documentary dependencies in your case.

Financial planning

How can intended parents afford surrogacy?

Most families do not fund a U.S. surrogacy journey from one source. A realistic plan starts with the full expected budget and payment schedule, then combines available resources without assuming that every expense is covered or refundable.

Short answer: common funding sources can include savings, employer family-building benefits, grants, personal financing and help from family. The right mix depends on eligibility, timing, repayment cost and how much financial risk remains after the first quoted fee.

Start with the real budget, not the headline price

Before applying for financing, build an itemized budget that separates surrogate compensation, agency or matching fees, IVF, legal work, insurance, escrow, travel and contingency costs. Use the 2026 U.S. surrogacy cost guide as the starting framework.

Common ways intended parents fund surrogacy

1. Savings and staged funding

Not every expense is due at the same time. A journey can include deposits, matching fees, escrow funding, legal invoices, clinic payments and pregnancy-related expenses at different milestones. Understanding timing can reduce the amount of cash that must be available on day one.

2. Employer fertility or family-building benefits

Some employers reimburse eligible fertility, adoption or surrogacy expenses. Benefits differ widely: one plan may cover IVF but not surrogate compensation, while another may include a broader family-building allowance. Confirm eligible categories, lifetime caps, documentation requirements and whether reimbursements are taxable in your circumstances.

3. Grants

Some nonprofit and family-building programs offer grants. Eligibility can depend on residence, diagnosis, family structure, financial need or treatment plan. Treat grants as uncertain until formally awarded rather than as guaranteed budget funds.

4. Personal financing

Some intended parents use personal loans, home-equity borrowing, lines of credit or fertility-focused financing products. Compare total repayment cost, interest rate, fees, term, prepayment rules and whether borrowing creates pressure if the journey costs more or takes longer than expected.

5. Family support or crowdfunding

Family contributions or crowdfunding can supplement other sources, but they should not replace a contingency plan. Intended parents should also consider privacy, tax and documentation questions where relevant.

Match financing to payment milestones

A useful financing plan maps each funding source to the actual payment schedule. For example, agency fees, clinic costs and escrow funding may be due at different stages. Ask for milestone-based payment schedules before committing to financing.

Plan for costs that can repeat

Failed transfers, additional medication, insurance changes or a new match can increase the total journey cost. The surrogacy rematch cost guide explains why the lowest initial quote may not be the safest financing target.

Questions to ask before borrowing

  1. What is the full expected journey budget, including exclusions?
  2. Which payments are refundable and which become earned?
  3. When is each major payment due?
  4. What contingency reserve will remain after closing costs or loan fees?
  5. How would one failed transfer or rematch affect the budget?
  6. What is the total interest and fee cost if repayment takes the full term?
  7. Can the loan be prepaid without penalty?
  8. Are employer reimbursements paid before or after the expense?

Ways to reduce financial uncertainty

Compare provider scope and candidate access before paying large non-refundable fees. Review agency fee structures, how to compare agencies, and whether a more independent model changes which coordination services you pay for.

Surrogacy agency fees in 2026

Agency fees can be a substantial line item in a U.S. surrogacy journey, but fee structures differ widely. The meaningful comparison is what each fee includes, when it is earned, and what remains outside the quote.

Updated August 2026. Published pricing and service models vary by provider.

Quick answer

Many U.S. surrogacy agencies publish agency or case-management fees in the tens of thousands of dollars, but the labels are not standardized. One provider's “agency fee” may include recruitment and matching, while another may charge those separately. Always compare itemized scopes rather than headline fees alone.

What an agency fee may cover

  • Surrogate recruitment and initial screening
  • Matching support
  • Coordination with fertility clinics and attorneys
  • Case management during contracts, transfer, pregnancy, and birth
  • Administrative support for insurance, escrow, travel, or logistics
  • Rematch support if a match does not proceed

What may be billed separately

  • Surrogate base compensation and allowances
  • IVF and embryo-transfer costs
  • Independent legal counsel
  • Health-insurance review or new coverage
  • Escrow administration
  • Travel and lost wages
  • Additional costs caused by rematch or medical complications

How to compare two proposals

Request an itemized estimate and mark each line as fixed, estimated, refundable, contingent, or excluded. Also ask when each payment becomes non-refundable and whether a rematch creates an additional fee.

Questions to ask

  • Is surrogate recruitment included?
  • Is matching included?
  • What happens financially if the first match ends?
  • Is case management included through birth?
  • Are insurance and escrow services included or only coordinated?
  • Which third-party fees are excluded?
  • Does the agency require exclusivity while you wait?

Surrogacy agency fees in 2026

Full-service agency fees commonly represent one of the largest non-medical line items in a U.S. surrogacy journey. The important question is not only the price — it is what the fee includes and what remains separate.

Updated August 2026. Published pricing varies widely by agency and service model.

Quick answer

Many full-service U.S. surrogacy agencies advertise fees in the tens of thousands of dollars. A proposal may include recruitment, screening coordination, matching, case management, and journey support, while legal, insurance, IVF, escrow, travel, and surrogate compensation are often separate.

What an agency fee may cover

  • Surrogate recruitment and initial screening
  • Matching support
  • Coordination with fertility clinics and attorneys
  • Case management during contracts, transfer, pregnancy, and birth
  • Support with insurance, escrow, travel, and logistics
  • Rematch policies if a match does not proceed

What is often billed separately

  • Surrogate base compensation and allowances
  • IVF and embryo-transfer costs
  • Independent legal counsel for both parties
  • Health-insurance review or new coverage
  • Escrow administration
  • Travel and lost wages
  • Unexpected medical or rematch costs

How to compare two agencies

Do not compare headline agency fees alone. Ask each provider for an itemized estimate and identify which costs are fixed, estimated, refundable, contingent, or excluded.

Questions to ask

  • Is matching included?
  • Is there a separate recruitment fee?
  • What happens if the first match fails?
  • Is case management included through birth?
  • Are insurance review and escrow coordination included?
  • Which third-party fees are not included?

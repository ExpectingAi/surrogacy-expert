Decision Guide

Find a surrogate first or choose an agency first?

Both approaches can work. Agency-first can centralize coordination; a surrogate-first or broader search can provide more visibility into candidate availability before committing to one provider.

Short answer: If your priority is having one team coordinate the process from the beginning, agency-first may be a better fit. If your priority is understanding candidate availability before making a larger commitment, a surrogate-first or multi-provider search may be worth considering.

Side-by-side comparison

Candidate accessOften limited to the agency's own recruitment pipelineCan include candidates from multiple sources or providers

The key question: what candidate access do you actually have?

Two agencies with similar fees can offer different matching experiences because recruitment pipelines, screening stages and candidate fit differ. Ask what candidates are actually available for your criteria, what stage of screening they have completed, how new candidates are recruited, and whether your agreement restricts searching elsewhere.

Finding a surrogate first does not eliminate screening

A profile or preliminary match is not medical clearance. Any candidate still needs the required records review, psychological evaluation, legal process, insurance review, and fertility-clinic approval before embryo transfer.

When agency-first can make sense

  • You want one accountable coordination structure from the beginning.
  • You are comfortable with the provider's candidate pipeline, fees and contract terms.
  • You prefer less hands-on coordination across separate vendors.

When a broader search can make sense

  • You want to understand candidate availability before a larger commitment.
  • You have experienced a waiting list or limited candidate fit.
  • You want to compare candidates from more than one source.
  • You already have some professional relationships in place and primarily need matching support.

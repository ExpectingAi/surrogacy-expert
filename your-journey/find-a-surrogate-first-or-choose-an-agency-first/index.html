Decision Guide

Find a surrogate first or choose an agency first?

Both paths can work. Agency-first offers a single coordinated process; surrogate-first can give intended parents more visibility and flexibility before committing to one provider.

Short answer: If your biggest concern is coordination, agency-first may be simpler. If your biggest concern is access to candidates, waiting time, or committing before seeing the available surrogate pool, a surrogate-first or multi-provider search can be worth considering.

Side-by-side comparison

IssueAgency firstSurrogate first / broader search
CoordinationOne team manages the processMay require choosing coordination after identifying a candidate
Candidate accessUsually limited to that agency's recruitment pipelineCan include multiple sources or providers
Upfront commitmentOften required before matchingMay allow more research before committing
Waiting timeDepends heavily on agency inventory and recruitmentBroader search can increase the number of candidates considered
ScreeningTypically standardized by agency and clinicMust still be completed properly before medical/legal clearance

The key question: how much inventory can you actually see?

Two agencies with similar fees can offer very different matching experiences if one has active, suitable candidates and the other relies on a waiting list. Ask how many medically prescreened candidates are actively available, how often new candidates are added, and whether you are restricted from searching elsewhere.

Finding a surrogate first does not eliminate screening

A profile or preliminary match is not medical clearance. Any candidate still needs the required records review, psychological evaluation, legal process, insurance review, and fertility-clinic approval before embryo transfer.

When agency-first makes sense

  • You want one accountable team from day one.
  • The agency can show a healthy recruitment pipeline.
  • You are comfortable with its fees and exclusivity terms.
  • You prefer less hands-on vendor coordination.

When a broader search makes sense

  • You do not want to commit before seeing candidate availability.
  • You have already experienced a long agency waiting list.
  • You want to compare candidates across more than one source.
  • You already have your clinic and attorney and need primarily matching support.

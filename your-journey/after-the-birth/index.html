What happens after a surrogacy birth

Hospital procedures, parentage documents and vital records differ by state, court order, hospital and family circumstances. Plan the post-birth process with counsel before delivery.

Hospital planning

Before delivery, confirm which legal documents the hospital should receive, who the hospital recognizes for newborn decisions, room and visitor arrangements, and discharge planning. Hospital procedures should be coordinated with the carrier's preferences and applicable legal documents. The hospital birth plan checklist can help organize those conversations.

Parentage

There is no single nationwide pre-birth-order process. Depending on the jurisdiction and facts, parentage may be addressed before birth, after birth, by operation of law, or through another court procedure. Your attorney should explain what order or documentation applies to your case and when it becomes effective. See our pre-birth vs. post-birth parentage order guide.

Birth certificate and vital records

The names that appear on an initial or final birth record depend on state law, the applicable parentage order and vital-records procedures. A court order does not always mean a final birth certificate is available immediately at delivery. Our surrogacy birth certificate and parentage guide explains the distinction between legal parentage, hospital paperwork and state vital records.

Discharge and travel

Medical discharge is determined by the newborn's clinical team and the hospital's procedures. If you must travel after birth, ask your attorney what legal documentation may be needed and separately confirm airline, passport, consular or other travel requirements that apply to your situation. International intended parents can use the post-birth travel documents guide to plan the separate legal, passport and destination-country steps.

The carrier's recovery

The carrier's postpartum care and recovery depend on the delivery and her individual medical circumstances. Compensation, reimbursements or post-delivery benefits should be handled according to the written agreement and applicable law, not a universal timeline.

Relationship after birth

Some intended parents and carriers remain in contact and others do not. The useful approach is to discuss expectations before the birth while allowing both sides to revisit them as circumstances change.

Starting a surrogacy journey: first steps

Before choosing a program or surrogate, understand your embryo situation, budget categories, legal constraints and the professional roles that may be needed.

Clarify your embryo plan

Some intended parents begin with embryos already created; others still need IVF, donor gametes or additional testing. This affects sequencing, cost exposure and when it makes sense to begin matching.

Build a category-based budget

A U.S. surrogacy budget can include surrogate compensation and reimbursements, agency or coordination fees, IVF and embryology, legal work, insurance and medical costs, escrow, travel, donor services and contingency expenses. Avoid relying on one universal total because the mix varies substantially by case.

Understand your legal situation early

Surrogacy and parentage law vary by state and continue to change. Before relying on a particular delivery state or contract structure, speak with counsel licensed in the relevant jurisdiction.

Know the professional roles

  • Fertility clinic: medical screening, embryo transfer and reproductive treatment.
  • Reproductive attorneys: separate legal advice for the carrier and intended parents, contract work and parentage planning.
  • Agency or coordinator: may provide recruitment, matching and case-management services depending on the model.
  • Mental-health professional: psychosocial evaluation and counseling consistent with clinic/program requirements.
  • Insurance professional: reviews actual policy documents and coverage risks.
  • Escrow provider: may administer financial disbursements according to the agreement.

Choose a matching model

Options include a full-service agency, a multi-provider profile source, professional referrals, a known carrier or an independent journey. The right model depends on how much recruitment, screening and coordination you want to manage yourself.

Plan for contingencies

Potential delays can include additional screening, unsuccessful embryo transfer, rematching, insurance changes or legal issues. A useful plan includes financial and scheduling flexibility rather than assuming every phase will follow a fixed timeline.

Pregnancy and birth in a surrogacy journey

The medical schedule is set by the carrier's clinicians, while communication, hospital planning and parentage preparation should be coordinated well before delivery.

Early pregnancy

The fertility clinic determines the timing of pregnancy testing, early monitoring and transition to obstetric care. Intended parents may receive updates or attend appointments if the carrier agrees, but the carrier remains the decision-maker for her own medical care.

Prenatal care and testing

The obstetric team recommends visits, imaging and screening based on the pregnancy and medical history. There is no surrogacy-specific universal schedule for when a particular scan or test must occur.

Communication and involvement

Discuss in advance which appointments intended parents hope to attend, how updates will be shared, and what privacy boundaries matter to the carrier. These expectations can evolve during pregnancy.

Legal preparation before delivery

Ask counsel what parentage process applies in the delivery jurisdiction. Depending on the state and facts, legal steps may occur before birth, after birth, by operation of law, or through another procedure. Do not assume a pre-birth order is available or required everywhere.

Hospital planning

Before delivery, coordinate with the hospital about legal documents, newborn logistics, room arrangements and visitor policies. The carrier's preferences and medical team govern decisions about labor and delivery.

Birth

At delivery, the carrier remains the source of consent for her medical care. Newborn custody, legal parentage and vital-record procedures depend on applicable law and any court order or documentation in place.

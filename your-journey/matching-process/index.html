How surrogate matching works

Matching is a mutual compatibility process, not simply a waiting-list position. Medical status, legal fit, compensation expectations, communication, and personal preferences can all affect whether a potential match moves forward.

What matching can involve

Different programs use different models. Some present intended-parent profiles to surrogates, some present surrogate profiles to intended parents, and some require interest from both sides before an introduction. A visible profile should not automatically be treated as medically or legally cleared unless the program says so.

Reviewing a profile

Useful information can include pregnancy and delivery history, screening status, location, compensation expectations, insurance status, communication preferences, and views on major journey decisions. The amount of information available before an introduction varies by program.

The first conversation

An introduction may happen by video, phone, or in another format agreed by the parties. The goal is to determine whether both sides want to continue. Topics often include communication style, intended-parent involvement, travel, delivery preferences, expectations after birth, and views on difficult medical decisions.

After both sides agree

What happens next depends on the program and what screening has already been completed. Medical review, psychosocial evaluation, insurance review, legal work, and clinic clearance may occur in different sequences. A match should not be treated as final medical clearance until the treating clinic has completed its required review.

How long does this take?

There is no universal matching or post-match timeline. Timing depends on candidate availability, screening status, responsiveness, legal and insurance issues, clinic scheduling, and the complexity of the match.

A strong match is one where both sides understand the practical and personal expectations and still want to proceed after the necessary professional reviews.

Surrogacy agency waiting lists: what intended parents should know

A waiting list can be a useful way to manage limited surrogate supply, but the phrase “waiting list” can mean very different things across agencies. Before paying, understand what your place in line actually represents.

Short answer

Do not evaluate a surrogacy program only by its advertised average wait. Ask how many intended parents are waiting, how many new qualified surrogates entered the program in the last 90 days, whether matching is strictly first-in-first-out, and whether your criteria could move you forward or backward.

How agency waiting lists usually work

Some agencies use a true queue. Others use compatibility matching, where the surrogate reviews intended-parent profiles and decides whom she wants to meet. In that model, your calendar position may matter less than compatibility.

Questions to ask before joining

  • How many intended parents are currently waiting?
  • How many qualified surrogates matched in the last three months?
  • Is the queue chronological or compatibility-based?
  • Can I see active profiles before paying the full program fee?
  • Is any part of the matching fee refundable?
  • Am I allowed to search through another provider at the same time?
  • What happens if a surrogate fails medical screening after we match?

Red flags

Be cautious when a provider quotes a short wait but will not explain how the number is calculated, counts applicants who have not completed screening as “available surrogates,” or requires a large non-refundable fee before giving any visibility into current candidate supply.

Alternatives to a single waiting list

Intended parents may compare several agencies, use a multi-provider profile source, work through professional referrals, or pursue an independent journey. The safest strategy is not necessarily the one with the shortest advertised wait; it is the one that gives you enough access and transparency without compromising screening.

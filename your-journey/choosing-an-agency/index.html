How to compare surrogacy agencies

Compare what each program actually provides, what you pay and when, how candidate access works, what happens after a failed match, and what restrictions apply before signing.

Start with scope, not the headline fee

Agency and program pricing varies widely because the included services vary. Ask for a written breakdown separating the provider's own fee from surrogate compensation, legal work, insurance, escrow, medical costs, travel, and contingent expenses. Use the 2026 surrogacy agency fees guide to compare fee structures and inclusions.

Questions worth asking

  1. What exactly is included in your fee? Ask which services can trigger additional charges.
  2. How does candidate access work? Ask whether profiles are already screened, whether you can review currently available surrogate profiles before paying a major fee, and who controls each candidate relationship.
  3. How is matching prioritized? A queue, compatibility model, surrogate choice, and intended-parent criteria can all produce different results.
  4. What happens if a match ends? Understand rematch terms, additional fees, and refund rules.
  5. Are there exclusivity restrictions? Confirm whether you may search or work with multiple surrogacy agencies at the same time.
  6. How is escrow handled? Ask who holds funds, what written instructions govern releases, and what controls apply.
  7. How are legal services arranged? Each party should understand who selects counsel and who pays the legal fees required for the journey.
  8. How is insurance reviewed? Ask whether the actual plan documents are reviewed before treatment and how uncovered costs are addressed.
  9. What reporting can you provide? Instead of relying on a single advertised “average wait,” ask for recent recruitment and matching activity and how the numbers are defined.

Do not treat inventory size as matching speed

A larger visible profile count does not automatically mean a faster match. Some profiles may be in early screening, unavailable, duplicated across sources, or harder to match because of medical, legal, geographic, compensation, or preference constraints. Compare this with the guide to surrogacy agency waiting lists and the guide to how long it can take to find a surrogate.

Which agencies are actually “best”?

There is no objective best agency for every intended parent. A useful comparison weighs candidate access, screening, recent recruitment, wait-time definitions, fees, rematch terms, exclusivity and support. Use our dedicated best surrogacy agencies in 2026 comparison framework rather than relying on a sponsored ranking alone.

Should you choose the agency first?

Not always. Some intended parents prefer to identify a promising surrogate before committing to one full-service agency. Others value having one provider manage recruitment and coordination from the beginning. Review find a surrogate first or choose an agency first before signing an exclusive agreement.

Red flags

Be cautious when a provider guarantees a match date, will not explain fees or refund terms, counts unscreened applicants as available surrogates, pressures you to sign before you understand exclusivity, or will not explain how funds are handled. See the surrogacy agency red flags checklist.

Professional memberships

Membership in an industry or family-building organization can provide context, but it should not be described as government approval or universal accreditation. Evaluate the actual program, contracts, professionals, and safeguards.

Choose based on transparent terms, quality of screening and coordination, and the actual matching model—not a headline fee or advertised waiting time.

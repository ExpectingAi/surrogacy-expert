Surrogacy legal fees: what intended parents are paying for

Legal fees are not simply the price of one contract. A U.S. journey may involve separate counsel, negotiation of the gestational-carrier agreement, parentage work and state-specific filings or procedures.

Updated August 2026. Surrogacy law varies by state. This page provides general educational information and is not legal advice.

Quick answer

The most useful way to compare legal quotes is by scope, not by one headline number. Ask which work is included, whether the surrogate's independent counsel is included in the estimate, whether parentage work is separate, and which filing, court or local-counsel costs may be additional.

Common legal cost categories

  • Intended-parent legal representation
  • Independent legal representation for the surrogate
  • Drafting and negotiating the gestational-carrier agreement
  • Review of compensation, expenses, insurance obligations and decision-making provisions
  • Pre-birth or post-birth parentage work depending on applicable law
  • Court filings, certified copies or local counsel where required
  • Additional work if the journey changes materially

Why separate counsel matters

The intended parents and surrogate have different rights, responsibilities and risks. Independent representation allows each side to receive advice from its own attorney before the agreement is signed. Intended parents should confirm whether the quote they receive includes or excludes the surrogate's counsel.

Why state matters

There is no single federal U.S. surrogacy framework. Contract requirements and parentage procedures differ by state, so a quote for one jurisdiction may not accurately predict another. Use our surrogacy-by-state guides as a starting point, then obtain advice from qualified counsel for the states relevant to the actual journey.

Questions to ask when comparing attorney quotes

  • Is contract drafting and negotiation included?
  • Is the surrogate's independent attorney included or separate?
  • Is parentage work included in the quoted fee?
  • Are court and filing fees included?
  • Could local counsel be required?
  • What events trigger additional legal fees?
  • Does the scope include insurance and escrow provisions in the agreement?

Legal costs belong inside the full budget

Legal work should be budgeted alongside insurance review and coverage, escrow, agency fees and the broader 2026 surrogacy cost plan. A low initial quote is less useful if essential parentage or second-party legal work is excluded.
